```{=html}
Extraction

Copying facts out of a document into the boxes of a table, so nobody has to retype them.

In this project: Each work order has 9 boxes: work order number, date, equipment, serial number, what broke, labor hours, parts cost, priority and warranty claim.

Field (box)

One box to fill in, such as "Parts cost".

In this project: 9 boxes x 50 documents = 450 boxes scored.

Answer key

The correct answers, written down at the moment the practice documents were made.

In this project: The AI and the plain rules never saw it. It is only used afterwards, to mark their work.

Precision (right when it answered)

Of the answers it gave, the share that were right. High precision means few wrong answers.

In this project: On new forms the AI gave 217 answers and 204 were right: 94.0%.

Recall (facts found)

Of the facts really in the documents, the share it found and got right. High recall means little is missed.

In this project: On new forms there were 222 facts; the AI found 204 (91.9%) and the plain rules 91 (41.0%).

Familiar vs new forms (seen vs unseen)

Familiar forms use the layouts the plain rules were written against. New forms use different labels, wording and date styles that neither method was tuned on.

In this project: Documents 1 to 25 are familiar; 26 to 50 are new. The new half is the fair test of "what happens when next month's forms look different".

Layout (template)

The shape of a document: a labelled form, a short table, or a free-text email or note.

In this project: 3 layouts, each in a familiar and a new style.

Plain rules

Hand-written search patterns, such as "take the text after Serial No.". Fast and predictable, but they break when the wording changes.

In this project: The baseline the AI has to beat, and its safety net when the AI fails.

Regex (regular expression)

A compact way to write a text search pattern, used to build the plain rules.

In this project: See extractor/rules.py.

LLM (large language model)

A program trained on a great deal of text that can read a document and answer in words. ChatGPT is a famous, very large one that runs in the cloud.

In this project: This project uses Gemma 3 with 4 billion parameters (gemma3:4b, about 3.3 GB), small enough to run on a laptop's ordinary processor.

Local AI

An AI model that runs on your own computer instead of a company's servers. Your documents do not have to travel anywhere to be read.

In this project: The model runs in Ollama on the same PC.

localhost

A computer's name for itself (also written 127.0.0.1). A program that only talks to localhost is talking to another program on the same machine.

In this project: The extractor refuses to send text to any address other than localhost, 127.0.0.1 or ::1, and a test checks that.

Ollama

A free program that downloads AI models once and then runs them on your own computer.

In this project: It needs the internet once, to download the model. Reading documents does not.

OCR (optical character recognition)

Turning a picture of text, such as a scan or a fax, into real text a computer can read.

In this project: This project does NOT do OCR yet. It reads PDFs that already contain text. Real scans would need an OCR step first.

Hallucination (invented answer)

When an AI confidently states something that is not in its source.

In this project: 3 invented dates in 450 boxes; 0 invented or wrong numbers in the 100 hours and cost boxes.

Validation (automatic checks)

Checks on every answer before it is kept: a real date, a known category, a sensible number.

In this project: If an answer fails, the AI is shown the error and tries again, up to 3 times (pydantic does the checking).

Fallback

A backup plan that takes over when the main method fails.

In this project: After 3 failed AI answers the plain rules answer instead, so one bad document never stops the batch. It happened once (doc_043).

Synthetic data

Realistic but invented data, made by a program so that no real person, client or company is involved.

In this project: All 50 work orders are SYNTHETIC, made by a seeded generator.

Public data

Records a government publishes for anyone to use.

In this project: FDA recall notices and NHTSA vehicle recalls. They test the AI on real-world text.

FDA

US Food and Drug Administration. It publishes food and drug recall notices.

In this project: 10 recall reasons scraped from one FDA page and sorted into categories by the AI.

NHTSA

US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration. It publishes vehicle safety recalls.

In this project: 35 recalls; NHTSA's own label for the broken part is the answer key. The AI matched 31.

WISP (written information security plan)

A document describing how an office protects client information: who can see it, where it is stored, how it is backed up and thrown away.

In this project: Tax preparers must keep one under the FTC Safeguards Rule. IRS Publication 5708 is a free template.

FTC Safeguards Rule

A US rule (16 CFR Part 314) requiring financial businesses, including tax preparers of any size, to protect customer information and keep a written security plan.

In this project: This site does not claim the tool makes anyone compliant. It can help an office describe the tool in its own plan.

ABA Formal Opinion 512

American Bar Association guidance (July 2024) on lawyers using generative AI, including getting a client's informed consent before putting their information into a self-learning AI tool.

In this project: Whether a local, non-learning model needs that consent is a legal question for the firm, not something this site answers.

DuckDB and CSV

DuckDB is a small database that lives in one file on your computer. A CSV file is a plain table that Excel and Power BI open.

In this project: The finished tables are written to both, on the same PC.
```
